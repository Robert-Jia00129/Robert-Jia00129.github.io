### :  This part is all about you. Tell us about something you’ve done — academically or personally — and what you’ve learned from it. Was it a success or a challenge? Did it represent a turning point in your life? How did this particular moment in your life influence you, and how will it continue to influence you as you pursue your college education?  


When I first arrived at the University of Minnesota from Shanghai, I found myself immersed in a new world, brimming with diverse cultures yet overshadowed by the daunting challenges of adapting to an unfamiliar environment. \ From course registrations to understanding cultural nuances, navigating the complexities of a new academic environment was overwhelming.

After a few days of sink or swim during my first week of college, I eventually found comfort and support when I joined the Chinese and international student communities.  Over the past year and half, I have found ways to use my technical and computer science skills to create more structured support services for international students and helping them to integrate with the wider university campus. 

One of the first projects I initiated was helping international students set up bank accounts and mobile services which can immediately allow them to feel functional in a new country. For example, I reached out to a local bank manager to coordinate the process of assisting international students.  Each day, I personally accompanied a small group of students to the bank. This allowed me to assist with any communication barriers between the international students and the bank staff, ensuring a smooth account setup process. I also provided logistical support for mobile service set-up. Given that many service providers were not within walking distance, I collaborated with local organizations to arrange volunteer drivers who would transport the students to the mobile service company and back. This initiative not only addressed safety concerns but also simplified the process for students, making it more accessible.

Finally, I revamped an old website that offered a comprehensive guide to help international students make a smooth transition. Our finished product covered everything from selecting courses and paying tuition to applying for a driver's license. To further complement the guide, I developed a WeChat Mini Program, revolutionizing how students connect and share information. It became a hub for finding classmates, exchanging secondhand goods, and neatly categorizing needs and interactions away from the chaos of general group chats.

The ripple effect of our efforts was profound. The once isolated and overwhelmed newcomers began to find their footing, thanks to the supportive network we nurtured. Our organization not only facilitated their initial survival needs but also fostered a vibrant, engaging community where students actively interacted and helped each other.  Using my problem-solving ability and technical skills to improve my community has been deeply fulfilling and rewarding.  Previously, I focused a lot of after school time on research, so this new experience of helping my peers with my strengths has been a turning point in allowing me to see what amazing impact I can make with my skills and now I am deeply attracted to community engagement. 

Through this process, I gained a valuable network of friends, strengthened my confidence to lead, and learned to be resourceful and innovative in finding new solutions to old problems. I also learned how to influence and collaborate with others by eloquently communicating the value of what I want to accomplish. I recognized that when a mission holds benefits for the collective, more individuals want to help and offer their perspectives for change or improvement.  At the end of the day, it’s great to work with like-minded peers who also care about communities as much as I do.  The more progress I made through connecting international students with all the wonderful things that happen on campus, the more I cared for a sense of community and belonging.  

As I look forward to expanding my academic horizons at a new school, I will continue to immerse myself in services and activities that enhance the integration of diverse student bodies and identify solutions for their challenges and concerns.  It’s my goal in life to continue to explore my abilities and develop new skills to help others, within my local community and greater society.  


### Tell us why you would like to attend the University of Wisconsin–Madison. In addition, please include why you are interested in studying the major(s) you have selected. If you selected undecided, please describe your areas of possible academic interest. 

My journey in computer science at the University of Minnesota has been inspiring. However, as I stand at the crossroads of my academic and professional aspirations, I find myself yearning for a new environment that aligns more closely with my burgeoning interests in AI/machine learning research and Linux kernel development. My academic pursuit in AI and machine learning has reached a plateau at my current school, having completed all available courses, including graduate-level classes. My ambition is to delve deeper into this field, contributing to cutting-edge research and innovation. I seek an academic environment such as U Wisconsin that offers advanced coursework and research opportunities in AI/machine learning that can challenge and expand my knowledge.

Additionally, through contributing to my school’s Kernel Development Club, I grew increasingly fascinated by Linux open-source community.  However, due to institutional constraints, I am unable to advance my research progress in this field.  Since UW-Madison has abundant opportunities in my interest area, I look forward to making greater achievements in it.

An area within AI that fascinates me is Natural Language Learning. In response to the repetitive inquiries and the overwhelming information demands within various student organizations, I spearheaded the development of an AI chatbot. This innovative solution was designed to streamline the information retrieval process, leveraging the comprehensive online documentation I had previously compiled. By training the chatbot with this knowledge base, it was adept at referencing and providing accurate, immediate answers to frequently asked questions. 

For queries beyond its existing knowledge, the chatbot was engineered to conduct online searches, delivering responses with direct links to its sources. This project not only significantly enhanced the efficiency of information dissemination among students but also deepened my interest in the field of Natural Language Processing (NLP). The experimental nature of integrating diverse chatbot features was particularly enlightening, further stimulating my curiosity and passion for exploring the depths of NLP technology.

This is why U Wisconsin’s renowned CS program, particularly in NLP and ML, deeply resonates with me. Courses like COMP SCI 769: Advanced Natural Language Processing and COMP SCI 545: Natural Language and Computing stand out as unique opportunities. They promise an advanced exploration into NLP, offering a perfect segue from my current understanding of formal language and automata theory to the intricate mechanics of large language models. Professor Hu's research area in Unifying LLMs with knowledge graphs for structured reasoning aligns greatly with my interest. These advanced NLP courses, not available at my current institution, represent the kind of academic depth and challenge I am eagerly seeking. UW’s emphasis on practical application combined with theoretical knowledge in NLP is precisely what I need to delve deeper into the mysteries of language processing technologies and fulfill my aspiration to innovate in this dynamic field.

Another great appeal to me about U Wisconsin is its integrative, purposeful learning upholding the Wisconsin Idea as the guiding principle.  As such, the school curriculum will not only strengthen my technical skills in CS but also allow me to tackle real world challenges through interdisciplinary lenses, holding values for social responsibility and global citizenship. I believe the College of Letters & Science will offer me the greatest opportunities to explore my interests and find ways to link them creatively to solve problems in our world.  

Asides from my strong desire to study at U Wisconsin’s esteemed Computer Science program, I also want to contribute to its vibrant learning community and student life. UW-Madison is known for its plethora of community engagement opportunities and over 1,000 student clubs and organizations.  I’m eager to bring my school spirit and leadership skills to contribute to some student clubs I would love to participate in.  Some of them include Community and Non-profit Leaders, Adventure Learning Programs, Artificial Intelligence Club, and Wisconsin’s International Students Association.
